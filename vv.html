Module 01

International Trade

Definition: A subfield of economics analyzing the exchange of capital, goods, and commercial services across geographic borders, governed by international regulations, tariffs, and macro supply chains.

Notes:

• Global trade maps show how values shift across

territories when port fees or tax blockades rise.

• Comparative advantage tells us why nations must

specialize instead of creating everything alone.

• Currencies fluctuate continuously based on trade

deficits and structural shipping supply pipelines.

Module 02

Management Science

Definition: The comprehensive use of mathematical modeling, algorithmic programming, and statistical simulation to resolve complex operational constraints and maximize organizational efficiency.

Notes:

• Linear optimization models require structured targets

like minimizing variable overhead or shipping waste.

• Shortest-path routing graphs assist container lines

in lowering fuel burns over global ocean systems.

• Stochastic planning allows networks to process safely

even when port bottlenecks disrupt timetables.

Tactile Pencil Mastery & Spatial Learning

Studying complex fields like International Trade and Management Science requires significant effort-based concentration. Writing equations or drawing logistics graphs with high-density graphite—such as premium Japanese Tombow pencils—slows down the thinking process intentionally. This mechanical pacing creates strong tactile memory pathways, prevents screen notification fatigue, and builds deep structural competence over complex technical systems.
