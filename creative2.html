Smarter Scrubber Framework & Middleman Risks

1. Case Study: The Smarter Scrubber Project

Led by Destin Sandlin (Smarter Every Day) and John Youngblood (JJGeorge), this project outlines an ambitious experiment in reviving local industry, eliminating safety hazards, and prioritizing long-term durability.

Primary Engineering & Strategic Objectives:

  • 100% Made-in-USA Supply Chain: Attempting to map the current state of domestic manufacturing, identifying lost industrial knowledge, and investing directly back into local toolmakers and suppliers.
  • Eliminating the Wire-Bristle Hazard: Bypassing dangerous, cheap bristles that break off into food by designing a safe alternative using interlocking, food-safe, welded stainless steel chainmail backed by high-temperature silicone.
  • Combatting the "Pandemic of Decay": Rejecting cheap, disposable consumer product trends by engineering a premium, robust, dishwasher-safe handle meant to last a lifetime.

The Systemic Reality (The Uphill Battle):

The project took over four years due to deep structural gaps in the domestic ecosystem. Destin discovered that many American injection molding companies no longer build their own tooling, routinely outsourcing mold creation overseas. To maintain a strictly domestic pipeline, they had to make the financially "illogical" choice to buy a CNC mill and cut the injection molds in-house themselves.

2. Structural Analysis: Managing Middleman Risks

The friction experienced in the Smarter Scrubber project highlights why organizations attempt disintermediation. However, as outlined in the repository file Managing Middleman Risks.pptx, bypassing external networks introduces intense trade-offs.

The Anatomy of Middleman Risks

Double Marginalization: Every layer of intermediation introduces accumulated markup layers, compressing margins and inflating final acquisition costs.

Opaque Value Chains: Intermediaries deliberately mask raw feedback loops and end-consumer relationships, creating an information blackout.

Agency Misalignment: Third-party brokers prioritize localized individual margins over long-term strategic alignments[cite: 5].
